The system

Five rules that survived

Each rule was found on one set of matches, then held up again in football it had never seen: men's club football and women's football, judged with the same space around the player.

C

Count

You win the ball. Count the opponents behind it.

3 or fewer back: attack now, two runners, first pass forward. 8 or more back: keep it.

each defender back
−11 to −19% value
S

Strike through

Near the box against a set defence, even under pressure.

Shoot. Don't pass sideways or back. Waiting doesn't produce a better chance later.

+0.04 to +0.11 xG
per decision
R

Run into space

Free in the final third, few defenders ahead.

Carry it forward. Don't take on a man or turn back.

+0.03 to +0.04 xG
P

Press the exposed

They receive near your box, unmarked, and few of you are behind the ball.

Press within two seconds instead of holding shape.

+0.008 xG
about once a match

Dead balls

Set pieces depend on team quality far less than open play.

Train corners and wide free kicks as your main weapon.

Why most patterns fail

Most patterns describe good moments, not good choices. Players go forward when there's space, so "going forward works" is mostly a description of space. We judged each choice against the same situation: defenders ahead, nearest opponent, teammates ahead and the shooting lane.

Football is also extremely noisy. A tactic worth four points a season would take a single club about 57 seasons of results to prove. That's why the work uses millions of decisions instead of match results.

Tested and rejected

  • Chase earlier when losing
  • Long throws into the box
  • Aim corners at the penalty spot
  • Find the opponent's weak spot
  • Fast passing tempo (it was just good teams)
  • Adapt to strict referees
  • AI-optimised in-game style switching
  • Simulator "chess engine" policies (overstate value 3–4×)
